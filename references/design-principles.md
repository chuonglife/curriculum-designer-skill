# Curriculum design principles (working digest)

Contents: 1. What a training program is · 2. Three design approaches · 3. Modules and subjects · 4. The 7-step process · 5. Selecting content · 6. Writing objectives · 7. Allocating hours · 8. Assessment · 9. Review checklist · 10. The alignment chain · Sources

## 1. What a training program is

A training program (curriculum) is the overall design of a training activity that may last a few hours or several years. It states the objectives, the content, the process and schedule, the methods and forms of delivery, how results are assessed, and what certificate is awarded (Wentling 1993). Tyler (1949) reduces it to four parts: objectives, content, method, evaluation. A module or subject syllabus is the most detailed level of the same design and is written by the people who teach it.

## 2. Three design approaches. Use the objective approach

| | Content approach | **Objective / outcome approach** | Developmental approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Focus | Amount of knowledge transmitted | Measurable output competence | Individual needs and interests |
| Content | Fixed in detail, reads like a textbook index | Only core content is fixed, chosen to reach the outcome | Flexible per learner |
| Method | Teacher transmits | Teacher organizes, learners work, varied formats | Teacher advises |
| Assessment | Recall and reproduction | Compared against the stated outcome, applied in real situations | Personal growth |

This skill always combines the objective approach with a modular structure, which is competency-based training (CBT): outcome-oriented, built on an analysis of the work, theory and practice integrated with practice dominant, and each lesson a learning situation that solves one concrete task.

Trade-offs to keep in mind. The objective approach makes quality easy to check and tells both teacher and learner what to aim for. Its weak point is that knowledge can become fragmented, so sequence lessons carefully and keep one orientation module for concepts.

## 3. Modules and subjects

- **Subject**: a body of knowledge arranged by the logic of a science, taught linearly. Systematic and thorough, but heavy, and it does not say what the learner will be able to do.
- **Module**: a complete learning unit that integrates knowledge, practical skill and professional attitude to produce one usable competency. Its features: relative independence, integration, stackability and credit transfer, separate assessment per module, learner-paced progress.
- Short programs and professional courses: modules only. Long programs: general subjects plus professional modules.

Rules for lessons inside a module: no overlap; a lesson may be reusable across programs; each lesson covers the knowledge and skill for one task completely. Lesson types: activity lessons (build a skill), technical information lessons (about tools, equipment, materials, diagrams, principles), and safety or regulation lessons.

## 4. The 7-step development process

**Preparation**
1. Situation and needs analysis: which occupation, who, how many, what competence is missing. Look at the organization, the job and the individual.
2. Occupational analysis (DACUM): break the occupation into **duties**, and each duty into **tasks**. A task qualifies when it has a clear start and end, a clear result (a product or a decision), can stand alone, can be assessed against a standard, and does not duplicate another task. DACUM rests on three premises: expert workers describe their job better than anyone else; a job is defined by the tasks expert workers perform; every task needs specific knowledge, skills, tools and attitudes.
3. Task analysis: for each task record the steps, the performance standard, the tools, the knowledge, skills and attitudes required, and the common errors.

**Design and evaluation**
4. Design the program structure: title, learners, duration, general objective, outcomes, module list with hours, module relationships, usage guidelines.
5. Write the detailed syllabus of every module.
6. Pilot or deliver.
7. Evaluate, revise, roll out.

In this skill, steps 1 to 3 are done in miniature: one table of duties and tasks written while thinking. Do not skip it. It is the basis for splitting modules and lessons, and it is what criterion 4 of the checklist looks for.

## 5. Selecting content

From the objectives and the duty and task table, list the knowledge and skills each task needs. Rate each item **must have / should have / nice to have**. Only the first two enter the program. Group by the logic of the job into modules, and order by the logic of learning into lessons.

## 6. Writing objectives and learning outcomes

**Formula**: (1) action verb + (2) object + (3) tools, conditions or context + (4) performance standard. Parts 1 and 2 are mandatory. Parts 3 and 4 are recommended at program and module level and expected at lesson level.

Lead-in: "On completing this [program / module / lesson], learners are able to:".

Three groups, matching national qualification frameworks: **Knowledge**, **Skills**, **Autonomy and responsibility**.

Level of expression: program objectives are general, module objectives are grouped, lesson objectives are detailed and measurable.

**Cognitive verbs by Bloom level** (Anderson and Krathwohl 2001). Match the verb to the level you actually intend:

| Level | Usable verbs |
|---|---|
| Remember | list, name, state, identify, define, describe, recall |
| Understand | explain, distinguish, compare, illustrate, summarize, interpret, classify |
| Apply | apply, use, carry out, operate, calculate, plan, set up, build |
| Analyze | analyze, determine, differentiate, diagnose, select, contrast |
| Evaluate | evaluate, critique, judge, justify, conclude, assess against criteria |
| Create | design, compose, develop, propose, organize, produce |

**Psychomotor skills** (Dave): imitate, follow instructions, perform precisely, coordinate fluently, automate and adapt. Verbs: handle, install, operate, assemble, record, edit, publish, test, calibrate.

**Attitude and autonomy** (Krathwohl): receive, respond, value, organize, characterize. Verbs: demonstrate, take initiative, comply with, cooperate, self-direct, take responsibility for, exercise care.

**Ways to state the performance standard**: accuracy or tolerance; time limit; quality level of the product; a rule, manual or industry standard to conform to; a percentage or count to reach; following the correct sequence of a procedure.

Good lesson objective (nursing): "Measure a patient's blood pressure during a routine ward round within 5 minutes; identify the correct patient; reading within 2 mmHg of the instructor's; record the result legibly on the chart; report out-of-range readings to the charge nurse immediately."

Weak versus strong:

- Weak: "Understand video editing." Strong: "Edit a 5 to 8 minute lecture video from a written script, with clean audio and at least 2 interaction points."
- Weak: "Know the platform rules." Strong: "List the 6 steps for uploading a course and the required image and promo video formats."

## 7. Allocating hours

- Units: one theory hour is 45 minutes; one practice, integrated or discussion hour is 60 minutes; one assessment hour is 60 minutes. Use the institution's own units when given.
- Reference ratio from the source teacher training program (248 required hours): 84 theory, 152 practice, 12 assessment. Roughly one third theory, two thirds practice. An orientation module may sit near 1:1. A skill module should have practice at least double theory.
- Assessment: 1 to 2 hours per module. A very short module (4 hours or less) may have none.
- Lesson length: 2 to 16 hours. Two-hour lessons are usually overviews or procedures. Ten-to-sixteen-hour lessons are practice lessons that end in a product.
- Live online sessions: keep one sitting to 90 to 120 minutes with a break, and at most 4 teaching hours a day. Count instructor presentation as theory. Count breakout work, role play, supervised practice on real tools and debriefs as practice. Put reading and recorded demos before the session as pre-work so live time goes to practice and feedback. State group size in the delivery conditions; 12 to 20 learners per facilitator is a workable range for skill practice.
- Self-paced video courses: theory is video watching time; practice is following along and doing assignments, usually 1.5 to 3 times the video length; assessment is quizzes and submitted work. Program hours are learner hours, not video minutes.
- Micro-courses (a few short videos, a total of learner time under about 8 hours): set `"time_unit": "minutes"` and count every time in whole minutes. Theory equals the video length of the lesson, practice is the pause tasks and the assignment (again 1.5 to 3 times the video), assessment is the quiz. Give every lesson `video_minutes`. The 2 to 16 hour lesson length and the 4 to 8 module count do not apply; 2 or more modules are enough, each still one usable competency.

## 8. Assessment

- Assessment content mirrors the module objectives, group by group.
- Forms: essay, oral exam, objective test, reflective report, project, product or skill demonstration scored with a rubric. Online: quiz, submitted assignment, capstone product.
- When writing assessment content, answer three questions: What can the learner do after this unit? To what standard at each step? Under what conditions?
- Four kinds of program evaluation: acceptance review, formative, summative, impact.

## 9. Review checklist (20 criteria, * marks the critical ones)

**A. Accuracy**
1. The duty and task map covers the duties and tasks of the role.
2. Every section of the format contains the required information.
3. Each section is written in the expected way.
4*. Content derives from the occupational and task analysis.
5. Technical information is correct.
6. Terminology is consistent.
7*. Any subjects follow the logic of the discipline and of learning.
8*. Modules integrate knowledge and skills following the logic of the job.

**B. Program structure**
9. General or mandatory components comply with applicable regulations.
10*. The program objective names the main occupational competencies of a graduate.
11*. The module set is sufficient to achieve the program objective.
12*. Assessment of learning results measures the program objective.
13. The link between modules and the duty and task map is shown.
14. The links among modules are shown.

**C. Module structure**
15*. Every module listed in the time-allocation table has a detailed syllabus.
16*. Module objectives state the main competencies gained.
17*. Module assessment measures the module objectives as written.
18*. The lessons are sufficient to achieve the module objectives.

**D. Lessons**
19. Lesson objectives have three components: performance, conditions, standard.
20*. Lesson content matches lesson objectives.

## 10. The alignment chain

Complete duty and task map, accurate task analysis, correct program objective, module set that reaches it, correctly written module objectives, module assessment that measures them, module content that reaches them, correctly written lesson objectives, lesson content that reaches them, program assessment that measures the program objective. A break anywhere in this chain is a design defect, even when every individual page looks fine.
